<section className="value-thesis" aria-labelledby="value-thesis-heading">

  <h2 id="value-thesis-heading">
    Make UI risk visible before it reaches a customer
  </h2>
  <p>
    Storybook makes important UI states reproducible. Chromatic turns those
    states into a shared, automated quality and review workflow. Together, they
    help teams understand what they support, test what can break, and decide
    whether a change is safe to ship.
  </p>
</section>

<section className="value-scenario" aria-labelledby="value-scenario-heading">

    </p>
  </div>
  <ul className="value-scenario__signals">

    <li>Accessibility problems surface during release hardening.</li>

<section className="value-product-pair" aria-label="The role of each product">
  <article>
    <span>Storybook</span>
    <h2>Make the state real</h2>
    <p>
      A story is a small, named, runnable example of UI. It gives the team a
      stable place to develop, document, and test a state without driving the
      entire application into position.
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li>Controls make inputs inspectable.</li>
      <li>MSW makes network states deterministic.</li>
      <li>
        Interaction and accessibility tests run where the state is already
        visible.
      </li>
    </ul>
    <p className="value-boundary">
      <strong>The boundary:</strong> local Storybook alone does not preserve a
      Git-aware visual history or give a remote team a shared review decision.
    </p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Chromatic</span>
    <h2>Make the change shared</h2>
    <p>
      Chromatic publishes the Storybook, captures its stories, compares them
      with accepted baselines, and puts the result next to the branch and commit
      that produced it.
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li>Visual tests catch presentation changes other tests miss.</li>
      <li>Branch builds give reviewers the same runnable evidence.</li>
      <li>UI Review records whether a change is intended.</li>
    </ul>
    <p className="value-boundary">
      <strong>The boundary:</strong> Chromatic cannot decide which customer
      states deserve stories. Thin stories still produce thin coverage.
    </p>
  </article>
</section>

<section className="value-loop" aria-labelledby="value-loop-heading">

    <p>

    </p>
  </div>
  <ol aria-label="Customer quality workflow">

## Translate product mechanics into customer outcomes

<div className="value-map">
  <article>
    <span>Hard-to-reach UI</span>
    <strong>Reproducible stories</strong>
    <p>Bugs and edge cases become visible, named, and easier to discuss.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Slow application loop</span>
    <strong>Isolated development</strong>
    <p>Teams get useful feedback without waiting on the rest of the app.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Tests miss appearance</span>
    <strong>Visual baselines</strong>
    <p>Layout and styling regressions become reviewable evidence.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Screenshots in chat</span>
    <strong>Branch builds and UI Review</strong>
    <p>Everyone inspects the same runnable build and recorded decision.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Late accessibility fixes</span>
    <strong>Tests beside the story</strong>
    <p>Common issues are caught closer to the code that introduced them.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <span>Coverage grows expensive</span>
    <strong>Modes and TurboSnap</strong>
    <p>Teams cover meaningful variants and optimize from evidence.</p>
  </article>
</div>

<section className="value-coverage" aria-labelledby="coverage-heading">
  <div>
    <span>The expansion conversation</span>
    <h2 id="coverage-heading">Talk about risk coverage, not snapshot volume</h2>
    <p>
      Anchor the recommendation in a blind spot: a consequential state, variant,
      or ownership boundary that can change without the right evidence. Snapshot
      volume follows from that choice; it is not the goal.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div>
    <blockquote>
      <p>
        “Which important states could change today without the right person
        seeing evidence?”
      </p>
    </blockquote>
    <ul>
      <li>
        Ask which viewports, themes, data states, and ownership boundaries
        matter.
      </li>
      <li>Recommend the smallest addition that closes a real gap.</li>
      <li>
        Do not expand coverage when the state has little consequence or the
        signal will be ignored.
      </li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>

<section
  className="value-role-grid"
  aria-label="Practice by customer conversation"
>
  <article className="value-role-card">
    <span>Presales lens</span>
    <h2>Prove that the workflow fits</h2>
    <p>
      Discover the consequence, show the shortest believable path from story to
      decision, and leave the customer with a concrete way to validate the fit.
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li>What breaks or slows down today?</li>
      <li>Who needs confidence before a change ships?</li>
      <li>What would a successful evaluation prove?</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
  <article className="value-role-card">
    <span>Postsales lens</span>
    <h2>Find the next useful coverage gap</h2>
    <p>
      Inspect what the customer already uses, locate a material blind spot, and
      recommend the smallest capability or practice that improves the signal.
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li>Which states are not represented yet?</li>
      <li>Where does review still happen outside the workflow?</li>
      <li>How will the team know the change helped?</li>
    </ul>
  </article>
</section>
