- 15 minutes: select the demo path and validate environments
- 20 minutes: rehearse once
- 20–30 minutes: deliver the mock call
- 15 minutes: debrief and capture feedback

## Prepare the call

Read the complete <a href="./?path=/docs/onboarding-reference-capstone-rubric--docs" target="_top">capstone brief and rubric</a>. Use the post-sales adoption criteria. Ask a teammate to play the customer or record an asynchronous demo and request timestamped feedback.

Before the demo:

```sh
pnpm check
pnpm dev
```

Also open your latest Chromatic branch build and CI run. Keep a known failing example or diff available
for the recovery moment, but do not leave the live branch in a broken state.

## Lead the call

1. Spend the first two to three minutes on discovery and reflect the customer's priorities back.
2. Choose a coherent path through Storybook, tests, Chromatic review, and CI. Do not tour every tab.
3. Include one multi-framework composition moment only if it supports the customer's discovery answers.
4. Diagnose one seeded failure or unexpected diff aloud.
5. Close with the next consequential coverage gap, the smallest change, and how the team will measure it.
6. State one boundary. Be explicit about what you would not recommend yet and why.

## Questions to think through

- Did discovery change the demo path?
- Which evidence supported the recommendation?
- Where did you start guessing instead of diagnosing?
- Did the next step fit the customer's ownership and adoption stage?

## How would you respond?

> “The workflow is useful, but our teams will not adopt another required check. What is the smallest
> change you would recommend?”

Start with the customer's current behavior. Name one consequential gap, the smallest change that
closes it, the owner, and the evidence that would show whether the change helped.

## Ask for specific feedback

- The reviewer completes every section of the capstone rubric.
- The reviewer identifies the strongest moment, one explanation to tighten, one technical gap, and a
  follow-up owner and date when needed.
- Your branch ends with `pnpm check` passing and no secret or generated output committed.

## Debrief while the call is fresh

Discuss or keep a brief note for each question:

- Where did you connect a product capability to a customer outcome?
- Where did you slip into feature-tour language?
- Did your recommendation fit the customer's adoption stage and ownership model?
- Which technical question made you least confident?
- What would you show differently for a small React-only team?
- What would you show differently for a large multi-framework platform team?

## Close the loop

Push the completed technical branch when your facilitator or team expects it. Do not create a
course-only commit for feedback.

Share the branch, Chromatic build, and feedback link with the course owner. Completion means you can
apply and explain the workflow. It does not mean you have memorized every option.
